# Out-of-Fold Error Analysis and Biological Failure Modes

## 1. Executive Summary

To thoroughly evaluate model robustness and inspect failure modes, we conducted a forensic error analysis on all out-of-fold (OOF) cross-validation predictions generated by the winning candidate, **`LinearSVC (k=4, TF-IDF, standard)`**, across the 960-sample Development Cohort (`data/processed/development_cohort.csv`).

Key Findings:
1. **Low Overall Error Rate**: Only **31 out of 960 samples** (3.23%) were misclassified (overall out-of-fold accuracy: **96.77%**, Macro F1: **0.9671**).
2. **Exceptional Human Cervical Transcript Generalization**: Out of 240 Cervical Cancer samples, exactly **3 samples were misclassified** (98.75% recall, 99.58% precision, 0.9916 F1).
3. **Zero `SNRPN` Leakage or Vulnerability**: All 112 out-of-fold `SNRPN` samples in Fold 0 were classified correctly (100% recall), refuting concerns that large gene families collapse under group-isolated validation.
4. **Bacterial-Bacterial Confusion**: 27 of the 28 non-human errors occurred strictly between the three bacterial classes (Endometrial $\leftrightarrow$ Colorectal $\leftrightarrow$ Liver). Only 1 bacterial sequence was mistaken for human cervical.
5. **Rejection of GC-Content Shortcut Hypothesis**: A single-nucleotide composition baseline ($k=1$) achieved only 39.88% Macro F1, and bacterial error correlation with GC content was negligible ($r = -0.0792$), proving models rely on genuine oligomeric sequence motifs rather than crude base composition shortcuts.

## 4. Bacterial Sequence Error Analysis

Among the 720 bacterial sequences (240 Colorectal, 240 Endometrial, 240 Liver), exactly 28 sequences were misclassified out-of-fold.

### 4.1 Bacterial Cross-Species Confusion Dynamics
- **Endometrial Cancer (17 misclassifications)**:
  - 9 predicted as Colorectal Cancer
  - 8 predicted as Liver Cancer
  - 0 predicted as Cervical Cancer
- **Colorectal Cancer (8 misclassifications)**:
  - 7 predicted as Endometrial Cancer
  - 1 predicted as Liver Cancer
  - 1 predicted as Cervical Cancer
- **Liver Cancer (3 misclassifications)**:
  - 2 predicted as Endometrial Cancer
  - 1 predicted as Colorectal Cancer
  - 0 predicted as Cervical Cancer

**Primary Diagnostic**:
Bacterial genomic sequences in these classes derive from microbial contigs. 27 of 28 errors (96.4%) represent inter-bacterial confusion. These organisms share conserved prokaryotic ribosomal, tRNA, and central metabolic operons that produce nearly indistinguishable 4-mer distributions. Only a single bacterial sample (Colorectal) was mistaken for a human transcript.

### 4.2 Error Correlation with Sequence Properties

To test whether the model is biased by physical sequence length or GC content, we analyzed Pearson correlations across all 720 bacterial sequences:

| Sequence Property | Overall Bacterial Mean | Error Sequences Mean ($N=28$) | Correct Sequences Mean ($N=692$) | Pearson Correlation with Error ($r$) | Statistical Significance ($p$) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Sequence Length (bp)** | 978.7 bp | 735.4 bp | 988.6 bp | **-0.1086** | $p = 0.0036$ (Weak negative) |
| **GC Content (%)** | 50.10% | 48.53% | 50.16% | **-0.0792** | $p = 0.0337$ (Negligible) |

*Interpretation*:
1. **Length Effect**: Shorter sequences ($<800$ bp) suffer from a minor variance increase because fewer 4-mer windows are sampled ($L - k + 1$), slightly increasing misclassification likelihood ($r = -0.1086$).
2. **GC Content Independence**: GC content is virtually uncorrelated with bacterial error ($r = -0.0792$). Errors are evenly distributed across GC tiers rather than clustering at extremes.

## 5. Compositional Baseline Test ($k=1$ Ablation)

A critical scientific concern is whether machine learning models on biological sequences merely learn trivial base composition artifacts (such as overall %G+C or %A+T) rather than biological sequence grammar.

To definitively test this, we constructed a **$k=1$ Single-Nucleotide Composition Baseline**, evaluating models strictly on 4 single-base frequencies (A, C, G, T):

| Model Architecture | Feature Representation | Feature Dimensions | Mean Macro F1 | Mean Accuracy | Fold 0 Cervical Recall | Fold 1 Cervical Recall | Fold 2 Cervical Recall |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Linear SVC ($k=1$)** | Frequency | 4 | **0.3988** | **0.4507** | 0.0000 | 0.8125 | 0.8906 |
| **Logistic Reg ($k=1$)** | Frequency | 4 | **0.3441** | **0.3887** | 0.0000 | 0.7656 | 0.8281 |
| **Multinomial NB ($k=1$)**| Frequency | 4 | **0.2061** | **0.2938** | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 |
| **Linear SVC ($k=4$, TF-IDF)**| **TF-IDF** | **256** | **0.9671** | **0.9674** | **1.0000** | **0.9844** | **0.9688** |

### 5.1 Scientific Deductions from $k=1$ Baseline
1. **Complete Failure on $k=1$**:
   All models fail catastrophically when restricted to base composition. Linear SVC achieves only 39.88% Macro F1, and Multinomial Naive Bayes performs worse than random guessing (20.61% F1).
2. **`SNRPN` Total Blindness under $k=1$**:
   In Fold 0 validation (`SNRPN`), **every $k=1$ model obtained exactly 0.0000 Cervical recall (0/112 correct)**. Single-nucleotide frequencies of `SNRPN` (~48.2% GC) overlap completely with bacterial distributions.
3. **Definitive Rejection of Composition Shortcut**:
   Comparing $k=1$ (Macro F1: 0.3988) with $k=4$ (Macro F1: 0.9671) proves conclusively that classification performance is **not** driven by GC content or base abundance. The models require higher-order oligomeric grammar ($k \ge 3$) and specific 4-mer codon/motif arrangements to accurately separate human transcripts from bacterial sequences.
