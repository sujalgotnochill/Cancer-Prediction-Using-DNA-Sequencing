About the DNA Sequence Classifier

Architectural specification, algorithmic foundations, biological provenance disclosures, and verified evaluation benchmarks of the frozen Phase 5 classification pipeline.

1. Project Overview & Scope

This web application serves as an educational demonstration and inspection tool for a computational genomics classification model developed across five engineering phases. The project began from a legacy machine learning codebase that originally claimed to classify cancer types (Cervical, Colorectal, Endometrial, and Liver) directly from short DNA sequences.

Rigorous forensic audits conducted during Phase 1 uncovered significant architectural defects in the legacy system: arbitrary train-test splits, data leakage, and deep biological provenance limitations in the underlying public benchmark dataset. The project was subsequently re-architected with reproducible preprocessing, group-aware cross-validation, cryptographic artifact versioning, and strict vault lockdown.

2. Biological Provenance & Dataset Limitations

Mandatory Disclosure

Crucial Scientific Reality: Not Clinically Validated

Forensic sequence alignment and NCBI BLAST audits demonstrated that the historical training dataset does not represent real human tumor biopsied transcriptomes:

  • Cervical Cancer sequences are actually human transcripts mapped to human chromosome 15.
  • Colorectal, Endometrial, and Liver sequences are bacterial genomic sequences derived from common microbial genomes.

Therefore, this model differentiates between human chromosomal transcript patterns and bacterial genome motifs, labeled historically under cancer designations. This application must never be interpreted as performing cancer diagnosis, cancer detection, or patient risk assessment.

3. Machine Learning Architecture

A. k-mer Tokenization (k = 4)

DNA nucleotide sequences are decomposed into overlapping substrings of fixed length k=4 using a stride of 1. With an alphabet of four canonical bases (A, C, G, T), the complete vocabulary space consists of \(4^4 = 256\) possible 4-mer tokens (e.g., AAAA, AAAC, ..., TTTT). During Phase 5 hyperparameter exploration, 4-mers provided the optimal balance between sequence context representation, computational efficiency, and robust generalization across non-overlapping gene families.

B. TF-IDF Frequency Representation

Term Frequency – Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF) is computed across the 256 4-mer vocabulary. Unlike raw k-mer counts, TF-IDF scales down ubiquitous background k-mers while accentuating distinct sub-sequences characteristic of individual source categories. Standard non-canonical representation was empirically verified to preserve strand-asymmetric biological motifs.

C. Linear Support Vector Classifier (LinearSVC)

The classifier utilizes a linear Support Vector Machine with coordinate descent optimization and squared hinge loss (\(C = 1.0\), max iterations 2,000). Linear hyperplanes separate the high-dimensional TF-IDF vectors in \(\mathbb{R}^{256}\).

Decision Scores vs. Probabilities

LinearSVC optimizes the margin between classes without producing calibrated posterior probabilities. Scores reported by this application are raw, signed perpendicular distances from the decision hyperplanes: \(f_k(x) = w_k^T x + b_k\). Positive values indicate membership on the positive side of the margin for class \(k\). These raw scores are intentionally not disguised as percentages or confidence metrics.

Scientific Observation: Cross-Validation vs. Holdout Discrepancy

As rigorously documented in the Phase 5 and 6 reports, the development cross-validation score (96.71% Macro F1) was notably higher than the final holdout score (91.05% Macro F1). The primary discrepancy originated from weaker Cervical recall (76.67%, 14 misclassifications) on the holdout cohort, demonstrating the generalization gap between the 23 development gene families and previously unseen sequences.
